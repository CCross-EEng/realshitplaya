Career objective

Draft written from Chris's résumé and lab work. Chris should rewrite this in his own voice: the rubric asks for his thoughts about his future goals, not a résumé objective.

The first time our balance controller caught the rotary pendulum and held it upright, control engineering stopped feeling abstract. A pole in the right half-plane is a pendulum falling over; moving that pole is what keeps it standing. I want a career built around that: taking something that wants to be unstable and making it dependable.

I'm most drawn to controls and automation engineering: designing and tuning feedback loops for motion systems, robotics and aerial vehicles, and programming the PLCs that run real production lines. Working on a GuardLogix cell showed me how much of industry depends on logic that has to be correct and safe every single cycle.

After graduation I'd like to join a team where I can learn from experienced controls engineers, take ownership of real hardware, and keep building depth in modeling, estimation and embedded implementation. Longer term, I want to carry a system from first equations to commissioning, and explain it clearly to the people who rely on it.

Rotary Inverted Pendulum Control

Spring 2026EE 4760 · Control System SimulationMATLABSimulinkQUARCQuanser SRV02
Description

A three-lab sequence that takes a Quanser rotary inverted pendulum from a blank model to a working swing-up-and-balance controller. We derived a four-state state-space model (arm angle θ, pendulum angle α and their rates) from the physical and actuator parameters. One open-loop pole came out in the right half-plane, which confirmed the upright pendulum is unstable on its own.

After confirming controllability (full-rank 4×4 controllability matrix), we designed a state-feedback gain K by pole placement through a companion-form transformation. The desired poles were −2.8 ± j2.86, −30 and −40, and we checked the result against MATLAB's place. We simulated in Simulink, then compiled with QUARC to run in real time on the hardware, with switch logic so balance control engages only near upright. The last lab added an energy-based swing-up controller that hands off cleanly to balance.

My role

Member of a lab team with Drew Bixler, Alex Kopeny and Alex Hardcopf (the swing-up lab was a combined six-person section). I worked through the modeling, pole-placement design, Simulink simulation and hardware runs. I also wrote the sign-convention check for the model lab, confirming that counter-clockwise rotation reads positive on the encoders, matching the math model.

Skills gained
  • State-space modeling of a nonlinear electromechanical system; stability from open-loop poles
  • Controllability analysis, companion-form transformation, pole placement
  • Validating a model against hardware: sign conventions, simulation vs. measured response
  • Real-time deployment with Simulink + QUARC and switching between controllers
  • Energy-based swing-up: measured potential energy 0.4193 J vs. 0.4199 J predicted

SoftSplitting work across a team, iterating over several sessions, documenting results against specs.

Quadcopter Stabilization & Navigation

Spring 2026EE 4760 · Control System SimulationMATLABPythonCrazyfliePID
Description

Two labs on a Crazyflie nano-quadcopter. First, parameter estimation: we flew a scripted routine, logged 19 states (position, velocity, Euler angles and rates, accelerations and the four rotor speeds), mapped rotor PWM to angular velocity (ω = 0.04 · PWM) and set up a least-squares estimate in MATLAB for the moments of inertia and the thrust and drag coefficients. A sixth parameter, rotor inertia Ir, came out at about −1.1 × 10⁻⁷, which confirmed it is negligible in the flight model.

Second, navigation: we tuned PID gains on x, y and z to keep step-response overshoot under 10% (z: Kp = 2, Ki = 0.5, Kd = 0), then flew a multi-waypoint trajectory. Raising Kp by one destabilized the quad almost immediately. Raising Kd by 0.3 still tracked, but the flight was choppier. Finally we configured proximity-sensor obstacle avoidance in Python, setting the trigger distance and retreat velocity.

My role

Member of a four-person team with Drew, Alex H and Alex K, working across the flight script, the MATLAB least-squares estimation, and the gain tuning and avoidance tests. We also changed the flight script to add a slow ascent and descent so the quad landed gently instead of cutting out mid-air.

Chris: name the pieces you personally drove.
Skills gained
  • System identification by least-squares regression from logged flight data
  • Finite-difference estimation of angular accelerations (T = 0.1 s)
  • PID tuning against an overshoot spec, and how sensitive stability is to Kp vs. Kd
  • Reading and modifying flight-control Python; safe hardware test practice

SoftDebugging hardware under time pressure, team communication, test planning.

PLC Factory Automation Cell

Spring 2026EE 4760 · Control System SimulationStudio 5000Ladder logicGuardLogix 5069-L320ERS2
Description

A three-lab build of a conveyor cell that sorts parts, assembles them and rejects anything incomplete, programmed in ladder logic on an Allen-Bradley Compact GuardLogix safety PLC. We mapped 17 standard and safety I/O points between sensors and solenoid actuators, then built a latched start/stop circuit for the belt and chain conveyors.

Sorting latches inductive and IR sensor states, so plastic rings are kicked into a hopper while metal pegs pass through. Up/down counters hold the hopper queue to five rings, and a rotary solenoid loads one ring at a time onto a passing peg. At the discharge, a one-shot and a capacitive sensor latch an "assembled" bit, so only lone pegs or rings are rejected. A dual-channel (DCS) emergency stop latches the conveyors off until the E-stop is released and the system is reset.

My role

Member of a four-person team with Alex H, Alex K and Drew, programming and testing on the live cell. The hopper count was incrementing before the solenoid had physically kicked the ring, which capped the queue at four. A one-second timer fixed that and also gave the rotary-solenoid logic the right timing. For rejects we chose latch logic over a full FIFO, then tested that it couldn't misfire.

Chris: note which rungs or features you wrote or debugged yourself.
Skills gained
  • Ladder logic: XIC/XIO, latching, one-shots, CTU/CTD counters, TOF timers
  • Standard vs. safety I/O; dual-channel stops with the fault-present bit
  • Finding race conditions between scan timing and physical actuator motion
  • Inductive, IR-reflective and capacitive sensing for part identification

SoftChoosing a simpler design deliberately, demonstrating safety behaviour, iterative testing.

Resources

CyMOS CMOS Fabrication Process

In progresscourse #CleanroomPhotolithographyOxidation / diffusion
Description

Fabricating a CMOS device wafer through a full six-mask cleanroom process: p-well, PMOS source/drain, NMOS source/drain, gate oxide, contact vias and metal. Every step is tracked on a process traveler alongside test-wafer measurements.

Field and gate oxides are grown by wet and dry thermal oxidation. The p-well and both source/drain regions are formed by solid-source boron and phosphorus deposition followed by drive-in diffusion. Predicted BOE etch times come from calibration test-wafer oxide thicknesses before each photolithography step. Aluminum contacts go down by e-beam evaporation, the interconnect is defined by PAN etch, and a sinter forms ohmic contacts.
