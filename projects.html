Rotary Inverted Pendulum Control

EE 4760 · Control System Simulation MATLABSimulinkQUARCQuanser SRV02
Description

A three-lab sequence that takes a Quanser rotary inverted pendulum from a blank model to a working swing-up-and-balance controller. We derived a four-state state-space model (arm angle θ, pendulum angle α and their rates) from the physical and actuator parameters. The open-loop poles came out with one in the right half-plane, which confirmed the upright pendulum is unstable on its own.

After confirming controllability (the 4×4 controllability matrix has full rank), we designed a state-feedback gain K by pole placement through a companion-form transformation. The desired poles were −2.8 ± j2.86, −30 and −40, and we checked the result against MATLAB's place routine. We simulated the controller in Simulink, then compiled it with QUARC to run in real time on the hardware. Switch logic engages balance control only near upright. The final lab added an energy-based swing-up controller that pumps the pendulum up from hanging and hands off cleanly to the balance controller.

My role

Member of a lab team with Drew Bixler, Alex Kopeny and Alex Hardcopf (the swing-up lab was a combined six-person section). I worked through the modeling, pole-placement design, Simulink simulation and hardware runs with the team. I wrote the sign-convention verification for the model lab, confirming that counter-clockwise arm and pendulum rotation read positive on the encoders, matching the math model.

III.b · Spring 2026

Quadcopter Stabilization & Navigation

EE 4760 · Control System Simulation MATLABPythonCrazyfliePID
Description

Two labs on a Bitcraze Crazyflie nano-quadcopter. First, parameter estimation: we flew a scripted routine, logged 19 states (position, velocity, Euler angles and rates, accelerations and the four rotor speeds), mapped rotor PWM to angular velocity (ω = 0.04 · PWM) and built a least-squares estimate in MATLAB to recover the moments of inertia and the thrust and drag coefficients. A sixth parameter, rotor inertia Ir, came out at about −1.1 × 10⁻⁷, which confirmed it is negligible in the flight model.

Second, navigation: we tuned PID gains on the x, y and z axes to keep step-response overshoot under 10% (z: Kp = 2, Ki = 0.5, Kd = 0), then flew a multi-waypoint trajectory. To probe stability limits we raised Kp by one, and the quad destabilized and crashed almost immediately. Raising Kd by 0.3 still tracked, but the flight was choppier. Finally we configured proximity-sensor obstacle avoidance in Python, setting the trigger distance and retreat velocity.

My role

Member of a four-person team with Drew, Alex H and Alex K. I worked across the flight script, the MATLAB least-squares estimation and the gain tuning and avoidance tests. The team also changed the flight script to add a slow ascent and descent so the quad landed gently instead of cutting out mid-air.

III.c · Spring 2026

PLC Factory Automation Cell

EE 4760 · Control System Simulation Studio 5000Ladder logicCompact GuardLogix 5069-L320ERS2
Description

A three-lab build of a conveyor cell that sorts parts, assembles them and rejects anything incomplete, all programmed in ladder logic on an Allen-Bradley Compact GuardLogix safety PLC. We mapped 17 standard and safety I/O points between the sensors and the solenoid actuators, then built a latched start/stop circuit for the belt and chain conveyors.

The sorting logic latches inductive and IR-reflective sensor states, so plastic rings are kicked into a hopper while metal pegs pass through. Up/down counters track the hopper queue and hold it to five rings. A rotary solenoid loads one ring at a time to meet a passing peg. At the discharge, a one-shot plus a capacitive sensor latch an "assembled" bit, and the reject solenoid fires only on lone pegs or rings. A dual-channel (DCS) emergency stop latches the conveyors off until the E-stop is released and the reset button is pressed.

My role

Member of a four-person team with Alex H, Alex K and Drew, programming and testing the rungs on the live cell. One problem we solved: the hopper count incremented before the solenoid had physically kicked the ring, so the queue capped at four. A one-second timer delaying the count fixed it, and the same delay made the rotary-solenoid logic fire at the right time. For reject handling we chose latch logic over a full FIFO, then tested that it couldn't misfire on the line.

III.d · In progress

CyMOS CMOS Fabrication Process

course # CleanroomPhotolithographyOxidation / diffusionE-beam evaporation
Description

Fabricating a working CMOS device wafer from bare silicon through a full six-mask cleanroom process: p-well, PMOS source/drain, NMOS source/drain, gate oxide, contact vias and metal. Every step is tracked on a process traveler alongside test-wafer measurements.

Field and gate oxides are grown by wet and dry thermal oxidation. The p-well and both source/drain regions are formed by solid-source boron and phosphorus deposition followed by drive-in diffusion. Before each photolithography step, predicted BOE etch times are calculated from calibration test-wafer oxide thicknesses. Aluminum contacts go down by electron-beam evaporation, the interconnect layer is defined by PAN etch, and a final sinter forms ohmic contacts.
